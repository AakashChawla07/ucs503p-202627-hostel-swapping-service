Hostel swap engine · benchmark

How far a swap has to reach before it helps anyone

A shorter swap chain is easier to agree on — fewer people have to say yes. It also throws away most of the matches. Measured on 300-student cohorts, raising the cap from 5 to 10 more than doubles the share of students who get an offer at all.

01 · Headline

Students who receive at least one executable offer

Mean across 10 cohorts. A student counts when at least one Pareto-improving chain within the cap includes them: nobody in that chain ends up worse off, and at least one person ends up better.

02 · Comparison

Three measures, three caps

% of cohort · bar = mean, whisker = ±1σ

Offered is how many students have an option. Served is how many can execute at once, since chains compete for the same rooms. Served barely moves from cap 8 to cap 10 — long chains crowd each other out, so the extra reach buys choice, not throughput.

03 · Spread

The gap survives the noise

offered % · one dot per seeded cohort

The three bands never overlap: the worst cap-10 cohort still beats the best cap-5 cohort by 10 points. Within a single cap, cohorts vary by up to 24 points — which is why this needs ten runs and not one.

04 · Scale

Why the cohort has to be large to see this

offered % · log scale on cohort size

At 20 students a chain of 10 is half the hostel, so the cap barely binds and all three settings land within 5 points of each other. By 300 students the cap-5 and cap-10 lines are 44 points apart. A small demo cohort would have hidden the effect entirely.

05 · Recommendation

Cap 8 is the operating point

Cap 8 reaches 58% of the cohort and 58.9% satisfaction. Cap 10 adds 14 points of reach for 2 points of satisfaction and two more people per chain who all have to agree.

Not built yet

The engine has no chain-length cap. candidate_chains does not limit length; these caps were applied to its output. Enforcing one needs a max_chain parameter through recommend_chains and lock_and_run.

Mind the default

A live round runs K = 50. On 300 students that reaches 58.8% at cap 10 against the 85.0% measured at K = 200, so quoting these figures beside a live demo needs the budget raised to match.

Cost

K = 200 on 300 students takes 51–68 s of search per cohort. The full study — 3 caps over 10 cohorts, sharing one search each — ran in 9.5 minutes, entirely in memory on synthetic pools.
